The lighthouse where Ansel grew up had two keepers, his mother and his father, and he was eleven before he understood that they had never once been awake at the same time. Nobody had told him this was strange. Nobody had told him anything. He only knew that they were doing the best they could to keep the lamp lit and the rest didn't matter.

<h1>Part 1. Background and upbringing</h1>
- The lighthouse. Its two keepers are a husband and wife, Ansel's parents, who split the night so cleanly they are never in the same room. Because whenever they are, they fight about what's the right way to keep the lighthouse running.
- Ansel grows up thinking a marriage is two people who hand off a lamp.
- He learns to climb the stairs and trim the wick himself. First as a game, then because it means all of them can sleep peacefully.
- The first time anyone praises him, it is for that.
- What he learns about love from watching them: it is a schedule, and it works best when the two people never meet.
- The rule he will carry out of this house: <span style="color:#CCCCCC">a keeper's job is to be needed by ships he will never meet.</span>

<h1>Part 2. The mainland</h1>
- He leaves for a city full of lights that don't need him.
- He is very good at everything, because being good is how you become necessary in a place where nobody asked you to come.
- He is proud of needing no one. He calls it independence. Nobody tells him it has another name.
- It's not that he's exactly selfish. But he has learnt that no one comes to protect / take care of his needs. So he'd help others only if it doesn't come at a cost of his peace.
- And yet, he's slowly becoming convinced that what he saw as love while growing up isn't the love he likes. He doesn't know what that is exactly so he's just observing others.
- What he decides it must be: the opposite of his parents. If theirs was two people who never met, real love must be one person giving everything to the other. Whoever he finds, he will keep the lamp for them every night and never once ask them to climb the stairs.

<h1>[wip] Part 3. The bigger light</h1>
- He meets someone who wants to build a light so tall it can be seen from every coast.
- Ansel signs on. Not because he wants the light, but because he wants to be the one who keeps it.
- Two years of building. He works nights, then days, then both. The same shift pattern he grew up inside, and he does not notice.
- The other builder is in it as if it is his life. Ansel is in it to be needed.
- Money arrives on words. Ships that promised to come never do.
- Somewhere in these two years he has found the one he was looking for. His partner, the single person he keeps the lamp for. One beat only: they offer to take a shift so he can sleep. He says no before they finish the sentence. He thinks this is what giving everything looks like. Do not expand it here. Do not return to it in this part.

<h1>[wip] Part 4. Dark</h1>
- The light fails. Not dramatically. Just a night it doesn't come on, and nobody is out there to miss it.
- A keeper with no lamp does not know what to do with his hands.
- He goes looking for something to tend. For the first time there is nothing.
- One beat only: his partner asks him what he needs. He genuinely does not know. The silence after the question is the darkest thing in this part, and it passes without comment.
- This is the bottom, and it is quiet.

<h1>[wip] Part 5. The ferry</h1>
- A warm country, alone, on his birthday. The first time nobody is expecting anything of him.
- Strangers with their own stories who need nothing from him, and he finds he can sit with them anyway.
- On a river ferry he notices the boat's own small lamp, pointed inward at the deck so the passengers can see their feet.
- It is the first light he can remember that was not for someone far away.
- One beat only: he thinks about his partner offering to take a shift, and understands for the first time that saying yes would have been a gift to them, not a failure of him.
- He does not fix anything. He just sits in it.
- End on him not yet changed, only aware. Still learning that being kept is not the same as being weak. That is where the story stops.
